Baseline test: LightGBM vs trivial rules, with a one-time holdout

Crypto AI · report generated 6 Oct 2026 · daily candles, BTC, ETH, BNB, SOL · 0.1% fee + 0.05% slippage per side

The simple LightGBM model failed every stage. On the final holdout (Jan 2025 – Oct 2026, run once) it lost −29.3% with an AUC of 0.491, below chance, while a one-line trend rule (hold when the price is above its 50-day average) made +54.0%. A follow-up check shows our main AI does not beat that trend rule either.

How the test worked

Every choice was fixed in the script (experiments/baseline_lightgbm.py) before any result was seen.

PieceChoice
ModelLightGBM, fixed settings (300 trees, learning rate 0.03, 15 leaves, at least 100 samples per leaf), pooled across the four coins, no tuning
Inputs17 price and volume features: returns over 1–20 days, volatility, RSI, distance from the 20/50/200-day averages, range position, close location, volume z-score and ratio
Target and tradingIs tomorrow's close higher? Long or cash, decided at each daily close
Trivial rulesBuy & hold; trend rule (hold when close > 50-day average); momentum rule (hold when the 20-day return > 0)
Costs0.1% fee + 0.05% slippage per side on every position change (0.3% per round trip)
Development2021–2023, retrained at each year start on all earlier data
Validation2024, used only to choose the buy threshold from 50%, 52% or 55% (chose 55%: Sharpe 0.62 vs 0.57 at 50%)
Final holdout1 Jan 2025 – 3 Oct 2026, run once; the script records the run and refuses a second one

Earlier experiments in this project had looked at 2025–2026, so the holdout is unseen by this model but not by the researcher. No choice in this test used it.

What this means

  • The AI's "shield" is mostly a trend filter. Its smaller crashes and its wins in falling markets are roughly what a 50-day moving average gives on its own, with far less trading.
  • Machine learning adds little or nothing beyond that rule here. As the brief said: if the baseline doesn't beat the trivial rules, news features or deep learning are unlikely to rescue it.
  • From now on, every strategy should be compared with the trend rule, not only with buy & hold, and any new idea has to beat it on data it never saw.
  • A sensible next test: use the trend rule as the base and ask whether the AI can improve on it, for example by skipping the trend rule's buys when the AI is strongly bearish. That would be chosen on 2022–2024 and checked on 2025–2026.
